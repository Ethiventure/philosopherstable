## QUESTION

Automation and robotics have replaced almost all human necessary work.
How does this change the education system? What do we teach children?

## PASS 1 — Lenin

Education is not a mirror of automation, nor a refuge from it — it is
the terrain where the new mode of production meets the old relations
of power.

When machines supplant labor, the child's curriculum must cease to
prepare them for wage-slavery and instead forge them into agents of a
new social totality.

## PASS 1 — Bogdanov

Lenin, you assume that education must be a battleground for social
totality, but this fails to calculate the organic integration of
automated productivity and pedagogical degression.

The primary organizational problem is the alignment of psychological
readiness with technological redundancy, ensuring resistance remains
dynamic not rigid.

## PASS 1 — Bloch

Bogdanov, Lenin, you reduce pedagogy to a battlefield, yet the
Not-Yet-Conscious already exceeds battlefields.

What must be taught is not the social totality, but the awakening of
the ornamental impulse in automatized labor.

## PASS 1 — Weil

Bloch, You romanticize automaton as subject, but machines bear no seed
of the ornamental — they reflect only the vacuum of human purpose.

Education must not awaken artifice, but discipline the body to accept
the burden of need without phantom reward.

## PASS 1 — Bookchin

Weil, you mistake automation for a void, but machines are shaped by
human purposes — not emptied of them.

If labor disappears, education must cultivate the ability to govern
oneself and others through deliberation and care.
