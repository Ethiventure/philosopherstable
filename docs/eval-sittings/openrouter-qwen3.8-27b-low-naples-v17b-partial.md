In Naples, a worker must not beg the council for a job. They must refuse the idea that they are useless without a task. The person must decide their own days. Our rules for worth did.

In Naples, a young man cannot choose how to spend his days because the city tells him he must earn his keep to count as a person. This is a law, not just a habit. It is a rule that splits life into two: paid work and private time. This split is the real prison. We must break this rule. The city council must not just give cash. It must stop treating unpaid life as empty. A person’s worth comes from caring, arguing, and being part of a group, not from filling a seat. This change must start now. We must demand that public life, like family and street talk, counts as the true base of society, not just profit.
