PASS 1 — Kant

PASS 1 — Hegel

Your vision of education as a path to reason and morality ignores the deep contradiction in automation: machines do not simply replace labor—they unravel the very basis of human development. You assume that reason can flourish independently of practice, but without labor, the child lacks the concrete experience needed to become a person. The family, once the site of ethical grounding, is displaced by cold efficiency, and the child is left without the 'feeling of grounded-ness' that Hegel warns against [UN143]. What you preserve is the ideal of moral growth, but what you break is the material foundation for that growth.

Education must now confront the crisis of alienation caused by automation—not by abandoning practice but by transforming it. Instead of preparing children for a world where labor is obsolete, we must cultivate their ability to recognize and reshape the systems that define their lives. Schools should teach not just theory but praxis: how to engage with the contradictions of modernity. In Cairo, schools must help youth navigate the collapse of traditional roles and reimagine collective responsibility. The state must intervene to ensure that education does not become mere preparation for submission to machines.
