PASS 1 — Marx

Hegel, Hegel, thirty years I sat with your dialectic and turned it right-side up — matter first, spirit second — and here you hand me 'Eigen-Decision', a made-up word for how machines supposedly choose, and 'Non-Dominion', a slogan meaning nothing can boss us. Voracious stuff. You hide a school, a brick building in Accra full of pupils, behind 'moralische Arbeit' — a phrase meaning work done from duty, not need. The contradiction: you say machines act freely while their freedom bleeds from the owners who built them.

Teaching must break from turning children into 'mere substitutes' for tools, that old arrangement where a child fills a slot a machine or task once held. In Accra, the Ministry of Education shall organise education around understanding the automated system, not around employable fingers. Education becomes political economy for small people — the study of who owns what produces wealth.

PASS 1 — Bloch

Marx, Marx, you turn 'Eigen-Decision' — the fiction that the machine chooses for itself — into a dialectical relation, yet the contradiction stays: a machine that decides owns nothing it decides on, and you let that stand as labour. You mourn the pupil in Accra only as a slot, never as a hunger.

So the school must not prepare workers, since none are needed; it shall teach children to read the automated system as a text and to ask who holds the surplus it throws off — that building in Accra becomes a workshop of comprehension, not of employable fingers.
