QUESTION
Automation and robotics have replaced almost all human necessary work. How does this change the education system? What do we teach children?

PASS 1 — Hegel

The child's education is not merely the transmission of knowledge, but the sublation of the immediate family bond into a mediated ethical life, where the child becomes a free and independent person through the dissolution of the family's immediate unity [UN144].

Automation, by removing the necessity of human labor, disrupts the traditional role of work as the medium through which the individual becomes a person, forcing a rethinking of how education prepares the child for ethical life and social independence.

Sources: [32] Lectures on the Philosophy of Right, 1819–1820 — G.W.F. Hegel; [29] Phenomenology of Spirit — G.W.F. Hegel

PASS 1 — Marx

You claim the child's education is the sublation of family into ethical life, but this merely conceals the real contradiction: the child is not becoming a person through work, but through the absence of it. What is sound in your observation is the dissolution of the family's immediate unity, but you ignore the new unity formed by capital's demand for non-labour. The child is not becoming free through ethical life, but through the absence of work, which is the new immediate unity. The real flaw in the question is not how to teach children, but who controls the education system when work is gone.

The underlying contradiction consists in the fact that education is no longer about preparing the child for work, but for non-work. What the problem actually turns on is the relation between the child and the machine. The child must be taught not to work, but to exist in a world where work is gone. The masses must act now to reclaim education from the machine, not to teach them to work, but to teach them to live. The new determination is that education must be reoriented from the production of labor-power to the reproduction of human life.

Sources: [13] Capital, Volume I — Karl Marx
