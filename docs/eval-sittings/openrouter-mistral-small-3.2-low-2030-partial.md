The visitor asks, "What are they talking about? Make it make sense."

On my account, the conversation is about the nature of work, machines, and the potential dangers of a society where machines take over tasks traditionally done by humans. The concern is not just about the loss of jobs, but about the possibility of a "rabble" emerging—a group of people who, due to a lack of meaningful work, become disconnected from society and its productive processes. This idea touches on the broader question of how we organise our collective power and how institutions shape and are shaped by our passions and affects.

For instance, imagine a city where most jobs have been automated. Many people find themselves without work, not because they are lazy, but because the machines are more efficient. These people might feel useless, leading to feelings of dejection and pride, as described in my work. The proud might delight in the company of those who flatter them, while the dejected might struggle to find their place in society. This scenario shows how the absence of work can lead to a breakdown in social cohesion, creating a "rabble" that is disconnected from the collective power of the community.

The key idea here is that work is not just about earning a living; it is about being part of a collective effort that gives people a sense of purpose and belonging. When machines take over work, we must ensure that people still have ways to contribute to society and feel connected to its productive processes. This is not just a practical issue but an ethical one, as it involves understanding the nature of human beings and their striving to persevere in their being.

To make this concrete, think of a community where people come together to work on projects that benefit everyone, even if those projects are not traditionally paid jobs. This could be anything from maintaining public spaces to caring for the elderly. The point is that work, in its broadest sense, is about being part of a collective effort that increases everyone's capacity to act and be affected.

So, the question is: How can we organise our society so that everyone has a meaningful role, even as machines take over more tasks? This is not just about finding jobs for people but about creating a society where everyone feels valued and connected.
