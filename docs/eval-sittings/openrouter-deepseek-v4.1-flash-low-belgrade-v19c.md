﻿THE DIALECTICAL CABINET

QUESTION
Automation and robotics have replaced almost all human necessary work. How does this change the education system? What do we teach children?

PASS 1 — Hegel

A mother in Berlin watches her child stack blocks with a screen that praises every wrong shape, because nothing real can be broken and nothing real can be feared. That soft praise teaches the child it is already complete, and so kills the urge to grow up. The schooling that removes all hardship and all need produces small gods who never leave the nest.

Education begins in a warm home, but it must end in a cold street. A child feels loved and safe, and also feels a sharp push to become something on its own. Punishment for a child is not about what is fair; it is about bending the will that has not yet learned to obey reason. So the teacher's job is not to fill empty heads with facts a machine already owns. The job is to break the child's private wilfulness and raise it into someone who can stand as a free person among other free persons. Knowing yourself as a person, not as a tool or a subject, was a great step for humanity, and it must be learned again in every generation .

Sources: [32] Lectures on the Philosophy of Right, 1819–1820 — G.W.F. Hegel

Not a spirit learning to be free — a kid whose fee gets paid.

In Belgrade, council-run workshops and kitchens: every child spends half the day at the machine and half at the book, from age nine, and no boss signs the certificate. Compulsory, public, and the same for the minister's kid and the Roma kid in Zemun. That is the minimum. The maximum comes when the machine does the work and the certificate stops being a wage ticket.

PASS 1 — Deleuze
