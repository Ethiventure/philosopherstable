## QUESTION

Automation and robotics have replaced almost all human necessary work.
How does this change the education system? What do we teach children?

## PASS 1 — Lenin

Automation and robotics replacing human labor alters the material base
of society, yet the superstructure — including education — must align
with this new reality. However, those who believe automation eliminates
the need for class-conscious organization are deluded. Education must
prepare individuals to wield technological mastery as a lever for
collective emancipation, not as a tool of alienation.

'Social being is independent of social consciousness,' and thus
educational systems must confront this contradiction head-on. Teaching
children to master machines means teaching them to transcend their
dependence on them — a lesson rooted in recognizing that 'the
sum-total of these changes in all their ramifications in the capitalist
world economy could not be grasped even by seventy Marxes.' This calls
for curricula grounded not in passive adaptation but active
intervention.

## PASS 1 — Bogdanov

Lenin, assumes automation removes the need for class-conscious
organization, but this ignores the deeper contradiction: when machines
perform necessary labor, education must prepare children to navigate a
world of shifting resistances and evolving social roles.

'Education must prepare individuals to wield technological mastery as a
lever for collective emancipation' — yes, but only if it teaches them
to anticipate resistance, adapt dynamically, and cultivate inner
resilience. As notes, 'stereotyped education in the image of the past
proves to be inadequate' under rapidly changing conditions. Training
must shift from rote discipline to adaptive intelligence.

## PASS 1 — Bookchin

Weil, Your insistence that automation renders education obsolete
conflates technical obsolescence with the necessity of cultivating
conscious engagement with societal structures — an oversight that
neglects the evolving roles of citizens in a decentralized, cooperative
society.

Education must now instill the ability to discern and reshape emerging
hierarchies within automated systems, ensuring that technological
advancement serves collective liberation rather than reinforcing
dominance.
