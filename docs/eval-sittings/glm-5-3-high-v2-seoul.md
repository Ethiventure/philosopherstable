## QUESTION

Automation and robotics have replaced almost all human necessary work.
How does this change the education system? What do we teach children?

## PASS 1 — Hegel

To celebrate liberation from labour misunderstands what labour
educatively was.

Education begins in the family's 'feeling of grounded-ness', exits
through discipline into civil society's 'system of needs'; mechanised
production cancels economic necessity yet Leaves Bildung — the forming
transition 'mere natural being' to recognised person — intact at
perhaps greater cost level previously absorbed incidentally by vocation.

## PASS 1 — Marx

Hegel, old teacher — you mourn Bildung as though vocation formed the
person. But the factory taught us: that formation was capital shaping
the child's 'living appendage' to 'the uniform and unceasing motion of
an automaton'. What you sanctify, I autopsy.

The question must be inverted: with necessary labour abolished,
Seoul's children need neither job-training nor your civil-society
discipline, but the 'germ of the education of the future' —
'productive labour with instruction and gymnastics' — labour now freely
chosen, socially organised, forming 'fully developed human beings'.
The Seoul district councils must expropriate the schools from the
credential-market and organise them around collective production.

## PASS 1 — Bloch

Marx, comrade of the Front — you have not entirely left the workshop,
and it betrays you. You would hand Seoul's children productive labour
with instruction, yet where machines devour every necessary task there
stands no labour left to teach; your polytechnic answers a world of
work the premise has already dissolved. You melt down the factory
school but rebuild it out of the factory's own debris — still training,
only for nobody.

And yet your deeper inheritance stands: the educator himself must be
educated, circumstances changed by men — that active side I carry
whole. So the shall: Seoul's district councils shall expropriate the
credential-market schools and organise children around 'alliance
technology', co-productive with nature — not dead automaton-motion, but
'delegation of toil to machines, centralized automation of the
inessential', freeing each child for the waking dream. There they learn
what remains when hunger-work ends: 'the constructional equipment of
externality', docta spes, the utopian surplus of every inherited
artwork and struggle — the Not-Yet-Conscious made teachable.

## PASS 2 — Marx

Hegel, you honour deliberate formative shaping — and that very premise
damns your federated polite citizens: in Seoul's e-waste yards a girl
still graded for obedience learns nothing of the 'living appendage' her
machines were. Virtue inculcated where expropriation stands exalts
servility into 'ceremonial conduct'.

The question wrenches itself to the factory floor: whose hands — the
automaton's or the community's — shape what the school 'combines with
instruction'? A class owning nothing is formed by nothing free,
however gracious its pedagogues.

## PASS 2 — Bloch

Marx — right that labour forms the young; so when Seoul's automata take
the toil, your polytechnic school, still bent toward production, grades
the e-waste girl for a workshop that no longer summons her. You stop
where the Theses stop: praxis without a Front.

What is left to form is the anticipatory being: teach the 'utopianly
superior' — decoded daydreams, natural law as fought-for dignity,
expression over ornament — so her hope becomes 'teachable', concretely
aimed.

## PASS 2 — Weil

Bloch — you teach her daydreams aimed at a future; but hope projected
forward is 'poison'. And your mine girl still cuts copper before dawn:
'iron does not' reproduce itself, the seams stay 'stained with blood'.
Necessity never left Seoul; you abolished it by decree.

What remains to teach in Seoul is 'attention' — the alone mind before
the collectivity, for 'collectivities do not think'; the robot school
must train each child, our e-waste niece included, to think alone what
the Beast cannot.

## PASS 2 — Bookchin

Weil — yes, the mine girl bleeding at dawn haunts me too; her attention
rescued my notion of parenting the young toward freedom. Yet you sever
thinking from acting: 'collectivities do not think'[?], but God forbid
children inherit solitude without power — your alone soul watches Seoul
and cannot vote.

So I press my own wound shut: this pedagogy must become a civic
arsenal, 'genuine town meeting' democracy[?] schooling.
